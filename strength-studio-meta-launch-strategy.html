Section 01

Reference model: @sgintlmarathon

What they did well, and what we're adapting for Strength Studio.

What SGIM did

~50 posts · New handle from scratch · Complete visual identity rollout

Launched a brand-new Instagram account for the rebranded BYD Singapore International Marathon (formerly Standard Chartered Singapore Marathon). Instead of migrating the old @sgmarathon account (78K followers), they started fresh at @sgintlmarathon — a signal of brand confidence. The grid was planned as a visual mosaic: alternating between bold typographic cards, full-bleed photography, and carousel explainers, all within a unified design system (lion mark, bold colour palette, consistent typography).

Their carousel style: image-heavy slides combining photography and typographic overlays, with no more than ~15 words per slide. The copy does its job in the caption. The slides are designed to be scanned in 2 seconds each — they communicate through visuals and short punchy statements, not paragraphs.

What we're adapting

We're a single-location personal training studio, not a national event with sponsor partners and athlete content. Our adaptation keeps the structural principles (grid-first planning, carousel-heavy mix, visual-first slides, coherent design system) but scales it to what's realistic for a pre-launch with limited photography assets and one person managing the account.

Key differences from SGIM: We have 12 posts (not 50), no professional photography yet (we'll lean on brand graphics + reno photos + AI renders), and we need to convert followers into $9.90 Training Assessment bookings — not just build awareness for a distant event.

Grid design principles

Alternating tone: No two adjacent cells share the same background treatment. Dark (Ironstone) and light (Canvas) posts alternate in a checkerboard pattern, with accent colours (#E05A2D Ember for conversion posts, Basalt for physio) placed strategically to draw the eye.

Photography vs graphic: Photo-led posts (reno progress, interior reveal) sit between designed graphic posts to break up the visual rhythm. Photo posts use a consistent warm-neutral grade.

Conversion posts are red: Posts 4 and 10 (the two $9.90 Assessment pushes) use the #E05A2D Ember background so they visually pop on the grid. When someone scrolls your profile, these are the posts their eye goes to first.

Bottom row anchors the brand: Posts 1–3 (announcement, explainer, reno start) are the "about us" row. New profile visitors scroll to the bottom first — this row should answer "What is this?" in 3 seconds.

Production note You can rearrange post order after publishing — Instagram now supports grid reordering via drag-and-drop. So if the grid doesn't land perfectly on first publish, adjust after.

Typography on slides

Headlines: Manrope · 700–800 weight · ALL CAPS · tight letter-spacing · 36–48pt equivalent · Canvas (#F4F0EB) on dark, Ironstone (#1F2225) on light

Body/supporting text: Manrope · 400–500 weight · sentence case · 18–22pt equivalent · Flint (#847B71) on light, rgba(255,255,255,0.7) on dark

Display accent (taglines, pull quotes): Instrument Serif · upright (non-italic) · slightly reduced letter-spacing · used sparingly for warmth

Copy rules (revised after studying SGIM carousel examples):

• Headlines: ≤8 words. Always ALL CAPS Manrope 700–800.

• Narrative slides (explaining a concept): total text ≤15 words including headline.

• Data/stat slides (tables, numbers, comparisons): can exceed 15 words when the extra text is structured visually — tables, bullet lists, before/after. The information should be scannable, not read as a paragraph.

Texture: Graphic slides (Templates A, B, C) should use a subtle texture overlay — grain, halftone dots, or a fine geometric pattern — over the flat brand colour. This stops slides looking like Canva defaults and creates a recognisable brand signature. SGIM uses a halftone dot pattern; Strength Studio should develop an equivalent (options: fine dot grid, subtle noise grain, or a geometric pattern derived from the logo mark).

Logo lockup: Strength Studio wordmark appears on Slide 1 (cover) and the final slide (CTA) of every carousel. Not on every frame — that clutters. Consistent bottom-centre or bottom-left placement.

Colour usage by content type

Training content: Ironstone backgrounds, Canvas text — the default brand treatment

Physio content: Basalt (#3D6A60) backgrounds — visually distinct from training, signals a different service

Conversion / CTA: #E05A2D Ember (#B03A2E) backgrounds — reserved for pricing and booking slides only

Audience-facing / explainers: Canvas backgrounds — lighter, more approachable for informational content

Photography: Warm-neutral grade, no heavy filters. Overlay text uses Template D treatment

Section 05

Stories cadence

2–4 Stories per day between feed posts. No designed creatives needed. Phone-native content. This is where the daily build narrative lives.

Story types

Build-out clips: 5-second phone videos of walls going up, equipment arriving, tradespeople at work. Raw, unedited.

Equipment reveals: One piece of gear at a time as installed. Ties to premium positioning.

Polls and questions: "What's holding you back from training?" / "Beginner or returning to lifting?" — engagement signals + audience research.

Countdown sticker: From July 25 onwards. Proven conversion mechanism — followers get a reminder notification when it expires.

Coach voice notes: 30-second audio clips from Bernard on programming philosophy or common training mistakes. Zero-camera format if Bernard is camera-shy.

Section 06

2026 Meta platform rules

Platform-specific rules for both IG and FB in 2026. Key changes since v1.

Hashtags: maximum 3 per post

[Certain] Instagram now limits hashtags to 3 per post, down from 30. Keywords in captions and on-slide text are now the primary discovery mechanism. Every post in this plan uses exactly 3 hashtags: one branded (#strengthstudio), one category (#personaltrainingsg or #physiotherapysg), one location (#eastcoastsg).

Carousels dominate engagement

[Certain] Carousels average 1.92% engagement (vs 0.50% Reels, 0.45% static images). Instagram resurfaces carousels to users who scrolled past, showing different slides — so a single carousel generates engagement over days, not hours. Adding music to carousels pushes them into the Reels feed for broader reach.

Grid reordering is live

[Certain] Instagram now supports drag-and-drop post reordering on the grid without deleting and re-uploading. This means you can fix the grid layout after publishing if the visual flow isn't right.

Slide count: 8–10 is optimal for educational carousels

[Likely] Most benchmarks recommend 8–10 slides for educational carousels. Our plan uses 4–6 slides per carousel instead — this is deliberate: shorter carousels with less copy per slide match the SGIM-style visual approach, where each slide is a designed card, not a text-heavy lesson. For a brand launch, visual impact per slide matters more than dwell time per carousel.

Facebook — no per-slide captions

[Certain] FB doesn't support per-slide captions on organic multi-image posts. Post 2's six IG slide captions collapse into one unified FB caption (see Section 04). Only FB carousel ads support per-card headlines — that's a different content model outside this plan.

[Likely] FB posts with hashtags underperform on business pages (multiple Buffer and HubSpot studies show this). All FB captions in this plan drop hashtags except Posts 1 and 12, which retain #StrengthStudio for brand trackability only.

[Guessing] Publishing to FB 2–4 hours after IG (rather than the same minute) tends to perform slightly better because it avoids Meta's cross-platform duplicate-content signals. Anecdotal from SG social media managers — Meta doesn't publish the exact mechanism. Recommended: IG post at scheduled time, FB post 2–3 hours later same day.

Cross-platform: original content rewarded, reposts penalised

[Certain] Instagram's algorithm heavily penalises reposted/recycled content — up to 80% reach reduction. Every post in this plan is original. Do not cross-post identical content from FB to IG without adaptation, and vice versa.

Section 07

Outbound engagement plan

The v1 plan had no engagement strategy. Posts alone don't build followers for a new account — you have to go to where your audience already is.

Daily routine (20 min)

Respond to every comment within 6 hours of posting. The algorithm decides how far to push a post in the first 24 hours based on engagement velocity.

Comment on 5–10 posts from local East Coast accounts daily. Targets: Siglap food accounts, East Coast running groups, local lifestyle pages, neighbouring businesses. Add genuine value — not "Great post!" — but a sentence that shows you're a real human in the neighbourhood.

Follow 10–15 relevant local accounts per day during pre-launch. Running clubs, cycling groups, yoga studios, local physio clinics, East Coast community pages.

Partner seeding

Identify 3–5 local businesses for reciprocal Story mentions or grid features during the first two weeks. Think: the coffee shop down the road, the nearby wellness or beauty studio, the neighbourhood florist. Not fitness competitors — complementary businesses whose audiences overlap with yours.
