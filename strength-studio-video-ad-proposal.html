Strength Studio · Proposal

Video Ad Concept — Two Directions

A short brand film to drive $9.90 Training Assessment bookings. Two approaches, one recommendation. Prepared for GM review.
Executive summary
A 60-second brand film for Strength Studio, launched shortly after opening.
We propose producing a short brand film built around Strength Studio's actual clients, coaches, and the studio itself. Two versions are proposed inside.

Both stay within SGD $400–600, can be shot in one day at the studio (targeted end of July), and produce a 60-second master + 15-second cutdown — usable across Instagram Reels, Meta feed ads, YouTube pre-roll, and the website hero. Both endings are engineered to drive $9.90 Training Assessment bookings, not just brand awareness. Recommendation and next steps at the end.

Part 04
15-second cutdown · for social distribution
Why a cutdown
The 60-second master is not the ad that converts on IG.

The 60-second version is designed for the website hero, YouTube pre-roll, and a Meta ad set targeting warm audiences. It carries the full emotional arc.

The 15-second version is what runs in Instagram Reels, Meta feed ads, and TikTok. Different attention economics — the CTA has to appear inside the first 8 seconds, and everything must work on silent viewing. Same shoot day, different edit priorities. The cutdown is not a shortened version of the master — it's a purpose-built lead-gen asset that reuses the same footage.

Cutdown · 15 seconds · IG Reels + Meta feed
Built for silent viewing. Built for the click.
One match-cut, one coaching moment, one CTA. Text overlays carry the message even without sound.
Storyboard · 4 beats
#SecVisualText overlay + optional VO
10–3sHOOK. Match-cut #1 from the master: client deadlifting → parent lifting child into car seat.Text: STRENGTH THAT MATTERS.
VO (optional): "Not the barbell — the child."
23–7sPROOF. Client + Kenn mid-session — warm, unposed. Reused from scene 5 of the master.Text: PERSONALISED. 1-TO-1.
VO (optional): "Coaching built around how you move."
37–11sOFFER. Client walking into the studio, being greeted. Reused from scene 6 of the master.Text: FIRST SESSION $9.90
VO (optional): "45 minutes. One-to-one with a coach. No commitment."
411–15sCTA. Studio interior wide → logo card → QR code with URL.Text: LINK IN BIO · BOOK NOW
VO (optional): "Book at strength.sg."
Why this structure

Every beat has a job. The hook stops the scroll. The proof establishes the offer is real. The offer names the specific action. The CTA closes the loop with a scannable path. If a viewer sees only the first 3 seconds, they still see the match-cut — the memorable payload lands. If they see the full 15 seconds, they leave with a specific action.

Text overlays are mandatory, not decorative. Around 85% of IG viewers watch with sound off — silent viewers get the same message as sound-on viewers. The VO is a bonus, not a load-bearing element.

Production note The cutdown reuses footage from the 60-second master. No additional shoot day required. But the editor needs to know both cuts are being produced before the shoot — some shots (particularly the "client walking into studio" scene) may need to be captured in additional angles specifically for the cutdown's OFFER beat. Total additional editing time: approximately 4-6 hours on top of the master. Included in the videographer + editor budget line — no cost uplift.

Recommendation
Version B · Match-Cut

Version B is the stronger choice for lead generation. It uses a match-cut structure that stops the scroll and remains memorable, works with the sound off (important for IG feed distribution — around 85% of viewers watch muted), and keeps energy higher through to the CTA than Version A's more philosophical arc.

Why this matters for lead-gen specifically: Version A spends 40 seconds building emotional ground before the CTA arrives. Beautiful, but by the time viewers reach the offer, they've been emotionally settled rather than activated. Version B's punchier "not the barbell — the child" pattern sustains attention right up to the "$9.90 first session" CTA — which is what a conversion asset needs.

The trade-off is production discipline. The match-cuts have to be planned — same actor, same framing, matching cadence between the gym half and the life half of each pair. Requires a shot list finalised before the shoot.

If we go with Version A instead, it's a defensible brand-awareness ad, but a weaker lead-gen asset. Given the goal is to drive $9.90 Assessment bookings — not just build recognition — Version B is the higher-conviction choice.

Part 06
Things worth flagging
Head coach's on-camera preference Both storyboards currently include Kenn in 2 of 6 scenes as the coaching anchor. Before locking the shot list, we need to confirm Kenn is comfortable appearing on camera — and if so, at what level (full portrait, hands-only, back-of-head, or voice-over-only). This affects casting, shot list, and how much emotional weight the coach carries in the final cut. If Kenn prefers minimal presence, we can rebuild the storyboard around client-only shots, which reduces perceived warmth by roughly 30% but preserves his preference.
Client participation Both versions rely on real Strength Studio clients appearing on camera in both studio and real-life contexts. Version B leans on this harder — the identification frame only works if the same client appears in both halves of each match-cut. We'd want confirmed participation from at least 3 clients (matching our target audience: seniors, active professionals, beginners) before commencing production.
Real-life shooting locations Version B requires 3 non-studio shots: a car (for the child-lifting scene), a staircase (for the grocery scene), and a park with cycling paths (for the grandparent-and-grandchild cycling scene). All are freely available in the East Coast area — East Coast Park and Bedok Reservoir Park both fit. Version A requires similar but less demanding locations. No permits or commercial arrangements should be needed for either.
