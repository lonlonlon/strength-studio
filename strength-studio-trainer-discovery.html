Strength Studio

Lead Trainer Discovery Questionnaire

For Glenn (GM) to use during the onboarding meeting with the lead trainer / operator. Answers will directly feed into website copy, marketing materials, and service definitions.
How to use this document: Work through each section conversationally — don't just hand this over as a form. The best answers will come from follow-up questions and natural discussion. Record answers directly in this document or on a separate sheet. Sections are ordered by priority: Training Methodology first (most urgent for marketing), Trainer Profile last (needed for website but less time-sensitive).
Section 1
Training Philosophy & Methodology Training
Feeds into: /training page hero, positioning copy, and all marketing materials. This is the foundation — everything else builds on this.
1.1
In one or two sentences, how would you describe your training philosophy to a new client?
We're looking for the "elevator pitch" — what makes your approach different from a standard PT session. This will likely become the headline on the Training page.
Notes:
1.2
What training system or methodology do you follow? Is it a named system, or your own approach built from multiple influences?
Examples: functional training, powerlifting-based, corrective exercise-first, periodised programming, conjugate method, etc. If it's a blend, list the influences. This helps us position the studio's intellectual framework.
Notes:
1.3
What does a typical first session look like for a new client — from the moment they walk in?
Walk us through the experience step by step. This will shape the Movement Assessment content on the website and the "what to expect" messaging for first-timers.
Notes:
1.4
How do you structure a client's programme over time? What does the progression from week 1 to week 12 look like?
We're trying to understand the journey a client goes on — this maps directly to our Foundation (8 sessions), Momentum (16), and Mastery (24) package structure. Does that framework match how you actually programme?
Notes:
1.5
What tools, equipment, or assessments do you use that most PTs don't?
Anything that differentiates the experience — movement screens, specific equipment, apps, body composition tech, video analysis, etc. These become visual and copy assets for marketing.
Notes:
1.6
What do you explicitly NOT do? What fitness trends or approaches do you avoid, and why?
Knowing what we stand against is as important as what we stand for. This helps define the brand's boundaries — "evidence-based, not fitness trends" is a current positioning line. Does that resonate?

Section 2
Client Segments & Ideal Client Brand
Feeds into: "Who it's for" section on /training, ad targeting, and overall brand messaging tone.
2.1
Describe your ideal client — who do you do your best work with?
Age range, lifestyle, goals, mindset. Our brand strategy targets affluent professionals aged 35-60 in the East Coast area. Does that match your experience, or would you adjust it?
Notes:
2.2
What are the top 3-4 reasons clients typically come to you? What problem are they trying to solve?
These become the "use cases" on the website — e.g. post-injury rehab, age-related decline, weight management, athletic performance. Rank them by frequency.
Notes:
2.3
What's the biggest misconception new clients have when they first walk in? What do you find yourself correcting most often?
This is gold for marketing — addressing objections and misconceptions in our content before prospects even enquire.
Notes:
2.4
Is there a type of client you'd prefer NOT to take on?
This helps us filter our messaging. If the studio isn't for competitive bodybuilders or people just looking for a cheap gym, we should signal that early.
Notes:
Section 3
Service Delivery & Session Format Operations
Feeds into: Training track descriptions (Solo/Duo/Crew), package details, and Rezerv booking configuration.
3.1
How long is a standard training session? Does it vary by track (Solo vs Duo vs Crew)?
We've assumed 60 minutes in the brand strategy. Confirm or adjust.
Notes:
3.2
For Duo (2 pax) and Crew (3-4 pax) sessions — how do you programme for multiple clients at different levels in the same session?
Semi-private training is our primary revenue driver. We need to explain clearly on the website how personalisation is maintained in a group setting. What's your approach?
Notes:
3.3
What does the Movement Assessment actually involve? Walk us through the protocol.
This is positioned as the entry point for all new clients ($200 value, 60 minutes). We need enough detail to write compelling website copy explaining what happens and why it matters.
Notes:
3.4
How do you track client progress? What metrics or benchmarks do you use, and how often do you review?
Progress tracking is a key differentiator for premium studios. This feeds into the "what's included" section of our membership page and helps justify the price point.
Notes:
3.5
For the Programming Only service (remote clients) — what does the deliverable look like? How do check-ins and video reviews work in practice?
This is an ancillary revenue stream for experienced lifters who train independently. We need to know if you've done this before and what the workflow looks like.

Section 4
Physio Integration Physio
Feeds into: /physio page, dual-pillar brand messaging, and potential physio partnerships.
4.1
What's your background or experience with physiotherapy and rehabilitation? Do you hold any physio-adjacent certifications?
The studio's brand is built on Training + Physio as two integrated pillars. We need to understand your capability on the physio side to determine whether we need a partnered physiotherapist from day one or if your skills cover the initial offering.
Notes:
4.2
How do you currently handle clients with injuries or chronic pain? At what point do you refer out to a physio?
This defines the scope of our physio offering — whether we position it as "physio-informed training" (you handle it) or "integrated physio" (we bring in a specialist). Critical for regulatory and liability reasons too.
Notes:
4.3
If we partner with an external physiotherapist, how would you envision that collaboration working session-to-session?
Some studios have the physio do an assessment, then hand off to the trainer with a protocol. Others have the physio present for part of each session. What model works best?
Notes:
4.4
What corrective exercise or manual therapy techniques are you trained in and comfortable delivering?
Specific techniques we can list on the website — e.g. soft tissue release, dry needling, joint mobilisation, PNF stretching, myofascial release, etc.
Notes:
Section 5
Differentiation & USP Brand
Feeds into: Homepage positioning, tagline refinement, and overall competitive messaging.
5.1
If a prospective client asked "why should I train here instead of Surge, Strength Clinic, or Ultimate Performance?" — what would you say?
These are our closest competitors in Singapore. We need a clear, honest answer to this question for both sales conversations and marketing copy.
Notes:
5.2
What's one outcome or result you're most proud of with a past client?
A specific story we can reference (anonymised if needed) as a proof point. "Client came in with X, after Y weeks they achieved Z" — the kind of narrative that drives conversion.
Notes:
5.3
Our current tagline direction is "Where strength meets science." Does this resonate with how you see the studio's identity?
If it doesn't feel right, what would you change? The trainer's buy-in on positioning language is important — you'll be the one delivering on the promise.

Notes:

What social media platforms are you active on? Would you be comfortable appearing in short-form video content (Reels, Stories)?
