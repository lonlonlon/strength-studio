Training Philosophy & Methodology Training
Feeds into: /training page hero, positioning copy, and all marketing materials. This is the foundation — everything else builds on this.
1.1
What does a typical first session look like for a new client — from the moment they walk in?
Walk us through the experience step by step. This will shape the Movement Assessment content on the website and the "what to expect" messaging for first-timers.

The first session is about understanding the client, not exhausting them.

Goal Discovery (10–15 mins)

  • Discuss their goals, lifestyle, training history, and motivation.
  • Understand any previous injuries, medical conditions, or movement limitations.

Movement & Physical Assessment (15–20 mins)

  • Assess posture, mobility, balance, flexibility, and basic movement patterns.
  • Identify strengths, weaknesses, and areas that may require special attention.

Baseline Testing (10–15 mins)

  • Depending on goals, assess strength, endurance, balance, and functional movement.
  • Record baseline metrics to measure future progress.

Introductory Training Session (20–30 mins)

  • Teach proper exercise technique and movement fundamentals.
  • Introduce exercises tailored to the client's current ability level.
  • Focus on building confidence rather than pushing intensity.

Roadmap & Action Plan

  • Review findings and explain the recommended training approach.
  • Set realistic short-term and long-term goals.
  • Establish clear next steps and expectations.

Clients leave the first session with a clear understanding of where they are today, where they want to go, and exactly how we will get there.

What we test and what it measures:

  • Squat test with pyro box — Lower body strength and endurance (essential for getting out of a chair or climbing stairs)
  • 90/90 Hip rotation test / Rotation exercise with weights or cable — Measures client's mobility
  • Bicep curl with weights — Upper body strength
  • Loaded pin machines workout — so they can see why working out at the gym is so important
  • Legs Curls / Legs Extension
  • Seated Chest Press / Shoulder Press
  • Cable Triceps Extension / Biceps Curls
  • Treadmill cool-down — check cardiovascular endurance

How do you structure a client's programme over time? What does the progression from week 1 to week 12 look like?
We're trying to understand the journey a client goes on — this maps directly to our Foundation (8 sessions), Momentum (16), and Mastery (24) package structure. Does that framework match how you actually programme?

For Senior Clients (Week 1–12)

Weeks 1–4: Foundation & Assessment

  • Movement screening and posture assessment
  • Improve mobility, balance, and joint function
  • Build confidence with basic movement patterns
  • Focus on fall prevention and daily functional strength

Weeks 5–8: Strength & Stability

  • Progressive resistance training
  • Improve lower body strength for walking, stairs, and daily activities
  • Core stability and coordination exercises
  • Gradual increase in training intensity

Weeks 9–12: Functional Performance

  • More challenging strength exercises
  • Dynamic balance work
  • Improve endurance and independence
  • Tailored exercises based on personal goals (golf, travelling, playing with grandchildren, etc.)

For Female Clients

Weeks 1–4

  • Movement assessment
  • Establish exercise habits
  • Core activation and technique work
  • Improve mobility and posture

Weeks 5–8

  • Progressive strength training
  • Focus on body composition goals
  • Improve confidence with resistance training
  • Build metabolic conditioning

Weeks 9–12

  • Higher training volume and intensity
  • Strength and muscle-toning focus
  • Performance-based goals
  • Long-term sustainability and lifestyle integration

2.3
What's the biggest misconception new clients have when they first walk in? What do you find yourself correcting most often?
This is gold for marketing — addressing objections and misconceptions in our content before prospects even enquire.

One of the biggest misconceptions new clients have is that they need to train harder, sweat more, or spend hours in the gym to see results. In reality, consistency, proper programming, recovery, and nutrition are usually far more important than simply working harder.

Another common misconception is that personal training is only for weight loss. Many clients are surprised to learn that training can also improve strength, mobility, confidence, energy levels, posture, and long-term health.

What I find myself correcting most often is exercise technique and expectations. Many people focus on how much weight they lift rather than how well they move. I spend a lot of time helping clients understand that progress isn't just measured by the scale — it's also reflected in improved strength, better movement quality, reduced pain, increased confidence, and the ability to perform everyday activities more easily.

Most new clients think they need to train harder to get results. What I often teach them is that smart, consistent training beats extreme workouts. I also help them understand that progress is more than just weight loss — it's about getting stronger, moving better, feeling healthier, and building habits that last.
